# Naming: replacing "Dog Schedule"

Owner: marketer · Round 1 · 2026-10-13 · Status: **shortlist for the user to decide**. Nothing registered, and `app.json` is unchanged.

> "Dog Schedule" stays the working name until the user picks one and a lawyer clears it. In this doc, "no conflicts found" means only that the checks listed below found nothing. It does **not** mean a name is available or legally clear.

## 1. Brief
- **Short.** One word or two short words. The home-screen label is ≤12 characters so it doesn't truncate.
- **Works in Greek and English.** A Greek grandparent should read it right from the Latin letters, and it must not mean anything awkward in Greek.
- **Ownable.** Not a plain dictionary phrase like "Dog Schedule".
- **Fits the idea.** The family shares the dog's day: claim → done, and nobody double-feeds. It should sit well with the paw-check icon ("dog task, done") and brand green #2F6B4F.
- **Distinct.** No confusion with competitors (especially **Pawlo**, plus Pawly, PawPlan, Pawday and the rest of the "Paw-" crowd) or with big pet brands.
- **Markets.** Greece and Cyprus first, then EU, UK and US with English listings ([mvp-scope.md](../business/mvp-scope.md)).

**Phonetics rule we added during the round:** Greek has no native *w* sound, and readers often say the English *aw* as "άου". Names built on **Paw-**, **Woof-** or **Walk-** are therefore harder for Greek speakers. The "Paw-" space is also crowded and sits right next to Pawlo. We preferred letters that read the same in both languages (d, g, b, k, l, m, n, r, s, t, v, z, a, e, i, o).

## 4. Shortlist (ranked)

### 1. DogDibs (top pick)
- **Meaning:** "Dibs" is the word for claiming something first ("I call dibs on the evening walk"). The name *is* our one unique feature, **claim → done**, the thing no competitor does (see [positioning.md](positioning.md) §3). "Dog" places it in the store and in search.
- **Pronunciation:** EN /ˈdɒɡ dɪbz/, "DOG-dibs". It is spelled exactly as it sounds.
- **Greek:** The Latin letters read phonetically, so a Greek grandparent says "Ντογκ-ντιμπς" correctly with no guesswork. "Dibs" means nothing in Greek, which is neutral, not awkward. The Greek UI and onboarding can explain it in one line ("Κλείσε τη βόλτα πριν τη βγάλεις"). There is no need for a Greek-script name, though the brand can be written "DogDibs" in both languages.
- **Conflicts found:** none for "DogDibs" on the App Store (US, GR), on Google Play or on the web. The plain word "Dibs" is crowded (15+ apps, and **1stDibs**, a well-known marketplace brand), so the "Dog" prefix must always stay attached. dogdibs.com is a parked domain, possibly for sale (⚠ not checked). The .app and .gr domains returned no DNS. ⚠ EUIPO/TMview, Greek and Cypriot national marks, and social handles were not checked.
- **Store name:** `DogDibs: Family Dog Routine` (27)
- **Subtitle EN:** `Call dibs on walks & meals` (26). Alternative: `Walks, meals & meds, together` (29)
- **Subtitle EL:** `Ποιος βγάζει τον σκύλο σήμερα;` (30). Alternative: `Κλείσε τη βόλτα, τσέκαρέ τη` (27)
- **Home-screen label:** `DogDibs` (7)
- **Icon fit:** strong. The paw-check reads as "dibs taken, task done". Tagline idea: *Call dibs. Do it. Done.*
- **Risk:** Greek users must learn the word "dibs". The English slang is mildly informal and may feel childish to some adults (⚠ test with 5–10 Greek and English users).

### 2. Voltaki
- **Meaning:** βολτάκι ("vol-TA-ki") is the affectionate Greek for "a little walk", the word Greek families say to the dog ("πάμε βολτάκι;"). It ties straight to our one-liner, *"Who's walking the dog?"*. In English it is a coined word, which makes it ownable outside Greece.
- **Pronunciation:** EN /vɒlˈtɑːki/, "vol-TAH-kee". Greek: Βολτάκι.
- **Greek:** warm and instantly understood. Nothing awkward. ⚠ It literally covers only walks, not meals or meds; the subtitle has to carry the rest.
- **Conflicts found:** none on the App Store (US, GR) or Google Play. **However, voltaki.gr is a live Greek online shop named "Voltaki" that sells powerbanks and chargers** (class 9 goods, in our launch market), and **voltaki.com is a live Brazilian SaaS named "Voltaki"** (customer-loyalty software, class 42 territory). There is also a crowded "Volta/Voltaik" field (EV, scooters). We would need a different domain (e.g. voltaki.app, which returned no DNS, or getvoltaki.com). ⚠ EUIPO/TMview and Greek national marks were not checked. A lawyer must judge whether these uses block us.
- **Store name:** `Voltaki: Family Dog Routine` (27)
- **Subtitle EN:** `Who's walking the dog today?` (28)
- **Subtitle EL:** `Πάμε βολτάκι; Όλη η οικογένεια` (30)
- **Home-screen label:** `Voltaki` (7)
- **Icon fit:** good. A paw on the way out, ticked off when done. The paw-check needs no change.

### 3. Skilaki
- **Meaning:** σκυλάκι, "doggy" or "little dog", is the most affectionate everyday Greek word for a family dog. In English it is a coined, friendly-sounding word.
- **Pronunciation:** EN /skɪˈlɑːki/, "ski-LAH-kee". Greek: Σκυλάκι. ⚠ English readers may stress it as "SKILL-a-kee" or think of "ski".
- **Greek:** warm and natural. Nothing awkward. ⚠ Because it simply means "little dog", a Greek or EU examiner may treat it as **descriptive** for dog-care software. That would make it the weakest mark of the three in our home market.
- **Conflicts found:** none on the App Store (US, GR), Google Play or the web. skilaki.com shows only a bare "welcome" page (owner unknown). The .app and .gr domains returned no DNS. ⚠ EUIPO/TMview and social handles were not checked.
- **Store name:** `Skilaki: Family Dog Routine` (27)
- **Subtitle EN:** `Share your dog's daily routine` (30)
- **Subtitle EL:** `Το σκυλάκι σας, όλοι μαζί` (25)
- **Home-screen label:** `Skilaki` (7)
- **Icon fit:** fine, but neutral. The name carries warmth, not the check/claim idea.

**Why DogDibs first:** it is the only shortlisted name that says what makes us different (claiming), it reads correctly in both alphabets' phonetics, and it is the cleanest of the three in our checks. Voltaki has the most Greek charm, but it already has two live namesakes, one of them in Greece. Skilaki is clean but likely weak as a trademark in Greece. **Reserve:** Azor (charming in Greek, but AZOR® is a US drug brand and the name is used by several apps) and DogRota.

## 6. Next steps
1. **User picks one** of the three, or asks for round 2. Before deciding, say "DogDibs", "Voltaki" and "Skilaki" aloud to 5–10 people: Greek parents and grandparents, and English speakers.
2. **Lawyer clearance search** for the chosen name. Cover EUIPO plus Greek (OBI) and Cypriot national marks via TMview, in classes **9, 42** (and 35/44 as a precaution), plus UK and US for the later expansion. For Voltaki, the lawyer must also assess voltaki.gr and voltaki.com.
3. **Secure the name only after clearance:** register the domains (.app plus .com if obtainable, plus .gr), the Instagram, TikTok and Facebook handles, and reserve the app name in App Store Connect and Google Play Console. Nothing has been registered or bought.
4. **Then rename the display name only:** change `expo.name` in `app.json` (and the store listing names). **Do not change `slug`, `ios.bundleIdentifier` or `android.package`.** Changing them breaks builds, updates and store identity.
5. Update [positioning.md](positioning.md) §5 (name, promo text) and the Greek strings once the name is final.
